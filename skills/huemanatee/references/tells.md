# Full tell catalog

Read this before drafting anything longer than a few sentences. Density is what matters: two items from this file in one paragraph means rewrite the paragraph.

## Vocabulary ban list

Restructure the sentence when one appears. Do not synonym-swap.

**Verbs:** delve, dive into, leverage, harness, foster, garner, navigate (metaphorical), underscore, showcase, elevate, unlock, unleash, embark, empower, streamline, bolster, utilize (write "use"), align (metaphorical), resonate, revolutionize, supercharge, demystify, unpack, unveil, spearhead, champion (as verb), cultivate (metaphorical), propel

**Adjectives:** crucial, pivotal, vital, key (as filler), robust, seamless, comprehensive, intricate, meticulous, multifaceted, nuanced, holistic, transformative, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, game-changing, ever-evolving, dynamic (as filler), vibrant, bustling, breathtaking, invaluable, unparalleled, paramount, profound, compelling (as filler), actionable, impactful, innovative (as filler), essential (as filler)

**Nouns:** tapestry, testament, landscape (metaphorical), realm, journey (metaphorical), beacon, cornerstone, treasure trove, plethora, myriad, synergy, paradigm shift, game-changer, deep dive, insights (as filler), takeaways, ecosystem (outside biology/platform contexts), powerhouse, hub (as filler)

**Adverbs and transitions:** notably, importantly, interestingly, significantly, arguably, ultimately, essentially, moreover, furthermore, additionally, consequently, thus, hence, indeed, undoubtedly, seamlessly, effortlessly, truly, deeply, profoundly, remarkably

**Stock phrases:** plays a vital/crucial role, stands as a testament to, serves as, in the realm of, a rich tapestry of, rich cultural heritage, at the end of the day, when it comes to, it's important to note that, it's worth noting that, it goes without saying, needless to say, in a world where, in an era of, look no further, the bottom line, elevate your, take X to the next level, whether you're a X or a Y, continues to captivate, leaves a lasting impact, paving the way, shed light on, a double-edged sword, navigate the complexities of, unlock the potential, harness the power of, foster a culture of, actionable insights, key takeaways, more than just, best practices (as filler), from X to Y (scope inflation), the world of X

## Constructions, with rewrites

| Tell | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| It's not just X, it's Y. | Decide which you mean: "Y." Or: "X matters mostly because Y." |
| Not only A but also B. | "A. B too." Or: "A and B." |
| No X. No Y. Just Z. | "Z, without X or Y." |
| The result? Z. | "That produced Z." Or simply state Z. |
| While X is true, it's worth noting Y. (hedge sandwich) | Pick one clause: "X, although Y." One hedge maximum. |
| ..., highlighting the importance of Z. | New sentence: "That is why Z matters." Or delete. |
| ..., ensuring that Q. | "So Q." Or delete. |
| Whether you're a beginner or an expert, ... | Address the actual reader. Delete the frame. |
| From startups to enterprises, ... | Name who it is actually for. |

## Rhythm tells

- Uniform sentence length, everything 14–18 words. This is what "burstiness" detectors measure. Break it deliberately.
- Three-beat staccato used for punch: "Ship fast. Stay focused. Win." Once is a device; twice is a signature.
- Every sentence opening subject-first. Vary openings: adverbial, prepositional, fragment, conjunction.
- Perfect parallelism in every list. Humans write lumpy lists.
- Zero fragments, zero contractions in casual venues. Contract where a person speaking would.

## Structure tells

- Five-paragraph-essay skeleton on everything, regardless of genre.
- Intro that restates the request; conclusion that restates the body; conclusion paragraph at all in short pieces.
- Template section headers: "Challenges", "Future Prospects", "Benefits", "Conclusion", "Final Thoughts".
- Symmetric coverage: every subtopic gets the same word count no matter how much it matters.
- Signposting: "Let's explore", "Below, we'll cover", "This article will".

## Formatting tells

- Bold-term-colon bullets ("**Speed:** ..."), especially where the bolded term is reworded in the sentence after it.
- Bolding key phrases mid-sentence for emphasis.
- Headers on a piece under ~400 words. Bullets for content that is not actually a list.
- Title Case Headings. Emoji bullets or emoji in headers. Horizontal rules between every section.
- Tables for three facts. Markdown syntax pasted into venues that render plain text.

## Register tells

- Polished blandness: confident, authoritative tone carrying no concrete detail. If the tone says expert and the content says brochure, rewrite.
- Promotional admiration in neutral contexts ("stunning", "must-visit", "beloved").
- Symmetric both-sidesism; refusal to land on a position; interpretation injected where neutrality was requested.
- American English regardless of the audience's dialect.
- Forced casual overcorrection: performative sass, injected slang, fake typos. Also tells.

## Workflow artifacts — never leave in delivered text

"I hope this helps", "Let me know if you'd like", "Certainly!", "Great question", "As an AI", "As of my last update", "Here's the revised version:", unfilled placeholders in brackets, fragments of the prompt, regenerate/turn markers.

## Endings

Banned closers: "Only time will tell." "The journey is just beginning." "The future looks bright." "Exciting times ahead." "...for years to come." End on the sharpest remaining point instead. If nothing sharp remains, the piece was one paragraph too long.
